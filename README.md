# CISO-blackjack for Energy
This is a codeless-repo that describes the setup of CISO Blackjack, a roleplay game for CISOs
Check the wiki for all the original details on the game:
https://github.com/koenmatthys/CISO-blackjack/wiki#ciso-blackjack-wiki

The Energy version has the following changes: <br>
Role cards (besides CISO): Beredskapsleder (Emergency preparedness), OT Cyber and Maintenance Engineer, SAS vendor, NVE/Havtil<br>
Existing rolecards such as CFO, Board of Directors, CEO/Director, etc are still usable. <br>

Organisational cards will become sector-specific, and consist of: Minor local utility, Major regional utility, National player (Statkraft, Statnett), Oil and Gas company, Key vendor to Energy sector (SAS vendor, system integrator)

Situational cards are also altered to become specific for Energy sector: <br>

Negative booster cards:<br>
(1) The legacy dilemma: A critical Operational Technology (OT) system controlling a key regional substation is running on an unsupported OS. A new, exploitable vulnerability is discovered.<br>
(2) Remember Hydro: A non-critical administrative system is encrypted. The attackers claim they have pivoted into the SCADA network (which controls power flow), but there is no telemetry to prove or disprove it. No one can confirm if IT and OT are satisfactory segregated.<br>
(3) Geopolitical stakes: Targeted KraftCERT TI confirms that a state-sponsored actor has gained persistent access to the your energy backbone. No impact has been observed yet, but "sleeper" malware is detected.<br>
(4) Supply chain attack: A key operational vendor is hit by a cyberattack and revokes all system resources. No further information is provided, but all vendor focus is at responding to the incident. <br>
(5) Insider in the control room: Suspicious communication patterns are detected originating from a high-level engineer's workstation at the Control Room during the latest shifts.<br>

Positive booster cards: <br>
(1) Nailing the audit: Your regulatory audit (NVE/Havtil) concludes with zero "High" findings for the latest security audit and praises the company’s security stance<br>
(2) The Failed Phish (Cultural Win): A sophisticated, targeted spear-phishing email is sent to your company´s management. Instead of clicking, several CxO reports it to the SOC within a few minutes, allowing the team to block the domain globally before further spreading.<br>
(3) Sharing is caring: Another company in the energy sector shares a specific threat indicator through KraftCERT. Your security team finds the same IoC and closes the gap before an attack occurs<br>
